The dream

So why doesn't it exist yet?

Why it fails

Not a skill problem.
Agents are built on the wrong shape.

The actual work is the space between our artifacts and the world — pressing what's made against what's real until it holds. The picture leaves that space out entirely — and the world hands out no score to climb.

Wrong at every scale — fractally wrong — and the outermost ring, the world, isn't even in the circuit.

First principle

An open circuit carries no current.

Anything — a feature, a market, a company — is a circuit: what comes out returns to shape what goes in.

Work is a fractal of circuits, and the outermost ring is the world — nothing closes until it passes through it.

Why now

The model is a battery.

LLMs have absorbed every playbook ever written — code, design, markets, operations. Enormous charge, sitting still.

Extraction is the art — the circuit is the extractor.

What we're building

The machine that closes the circuit.

  • Thinks wide and deep. A slower circuit holds the whole opportunity space — approaches, risks, unknowns — feeding direction down.
  • Makes results real. A gate in front of the world — nothing ships until it's verified against the live system.
  • Right-sizes the run. Grows circuits, prunes what's finished — the run itself never tires.

Coherence isn't a property of the model, and "production-ready" isn't a property of code — both are manufactured by the shape.

Why it's new

Every other loop needs a scoreboard.
The world doesn't come with one.

The frontier of self-improving agents — Darwin Gödel Machine, AlphaEvolve, agent self-play — all learn by climbing a number they can measure. That works on a benchmark. A company has no benchmark.

So we had to build what a scoreboard normally provides — four instruments, built into the shape.

Anyone can loop a model. The edge is running the loop where there's nothing to measure — and not fooling yourself.

refs Darwin Gödel Machine, 2025 Multi-Agent Evolve, 2025
  • Time becomes the signal. With no score to chase, the run's own clock sets the ambition — the machine keeps widening the plan to fill the run, so it never declares a small victory and stops.
  • "Is it real?" is wired apart from "is there more?" Two separate circuits, on purpose — so the drive to keep going can never inflate the belief that it's done.
  • The thinking has to get used. Every plan the machine dreams is reconciled against what actually shipped — analysis nobody consumed doesn't count.
  • Nothing counts until it's wired into the world. "Written" isn't "working" — it counts only when the live system runs it.

Before you ask

The questions you're already asking.

"Isn't this just Claude in a while-loop?"

A while-loop drifts in minutes. A circuit verifies every step against the world — that's the 720 hours.

"What happens when the next model drops?"

Our ceiling rises for free. Anthropic sells the engine; we sell the self-driving car.

"Who's accountable when it runs alone?"

Today, humans hold the gate. Trust is earned run by run; we're raising to build the robustness that widens it.

"Doesn't it just burn tokens forever?"

Does a company ever finish? When one opportunity dries up, the machine finds the next — spend follows returns.

"Why won't the labs build this?"

They're winning the model war — anchored to today's users and their own stack. The shape is a different game.

"Where's the moat?"

Run-lessons baked into the shape — and every real business it runs adds data nobody else has.

"What about hallucinations?"

A hallucination is imagination without a gate. Inside ours it's a feature, not a bug.

"720 hours can't fit in a context window."

Let's leave some things for the secret sauce.
